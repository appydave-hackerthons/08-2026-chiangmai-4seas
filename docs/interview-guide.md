## Before you press record — say the slate

Out loud, first thing, every single time:

> **"Chiang Mai 4 Seas. Lineage two. Participant [first name]. Interview one."**

Change the lineage number per person: L2, L3, L4, L5. Say it **identically every time** — it's how
we find this recording later. Improvising the wording is what breaks it. Three seconds.

## The one rule

> **Ask about the last time it happened. Never ask what they want.**

Your words from the kickoff: *"tell me about the last time you struggled with X, how often."*
Hypotheticals get you invented answers. A real memory gets you a real problem.

**Do not ask "what app would you like?"** Hold the app question until a recurring pain is on the
table. If you ask early, you get their guess at a solution and you have to work backwards out of it.

## Who you actually want

Not the AI-saturated hackathon crowd — **the person who can't build it themselves.** The test you
set: someone who gets a link emailed the next morning and is genuinely glad. If they start pitching
you an architecture, you've got the wrong kind of answer; steer back to what went wrong last week.

## The sequence

**1 · Ground them** (2 min)
- Who are you, and what do you actually spend your days doing?

**2 · Find the friction** — stay here longest
- Walk me through last week. What took longer than it should have?
- What did you do more than once that felt like the same job twice?
- What did you have to chase someone for?

**3 · Pin one thing down** ← the load-bearing part
- Tell me about **the last time** that happened. When was it?
- **How often** does it hit you — daily, weekly, monthly?
- **How long** did it take you?
- What did you do instead — what's your workaround?
- Have you **paid** for anything to fix it? Tried something that didn't work?

**4 · Widen it**
- Who else does this hurt?
- What happens if it just stays broken?

**5 · Only now, the future**
- If this were handled, what would your morning look like?
- What would you want sitting in your inbox tomorrow?

## The four probes

Whenever they say something vague, pick one:

| They say | You ask |
|---|---|
| "It's a pain" | **How often?** |
| "It takes ages" | **How long, last time?** |
| "We always have to…" | **When was the most recent?** |
| "It's a bit messy" | **Can you show me?** |

## Don't

- **Lead them.** "So that must be frustrating?" — they'll agree to be polite. Ask "what happened next?"
- **Accept the first answer.** The first pain is usually the socially acceptable one. Ask again, differently.
- **Take a proposed solution as the problem.** If they say "I need a dashboard", ask what they'd do
  with it, and what they do today instead.
- **Fill silence.** Count to three. They'll keep talking, and the second half is the honest half.

## When to stop

You have enough when you can finish this sentence out loud:

> *"[Name] loses [how long] every [how often] to [the thing], and today they cope by [the workaround]."*

If you can't, you don't have a problem yet — you have a topic. Keep going.

## After

- Don't stop the recording until you've stepped away — the useful afterthought lands in the last
  thirty seconds
- One recording per person. Don't merge two people into one file
- Say the participant's lineage number out loud again at the end if the slate was rushed
