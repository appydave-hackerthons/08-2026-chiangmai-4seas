Answers — what is this thing?

Traces in, artifacts out, artifacts become traces

Two evidence sources enter from opposite ends and do different jobs. Captain's Log is upstream — it tells us what might be worth building. Angel Eye is downstream — it tells us what actually happened while it was being built. The output becomes new input, which is what makes this an engine rather than a pipeline.

The framing that must not be lost

This is not a router, a project-management tool, a prompt chain or a code generator. The claim is that people already produce valuable traces, and that opportunities can be discovered in them before anyone has formally requested anything.

Deliberate constraint

A pipeline never advances just because a file exists — every phase has a quality gate, and the ranking score organises attention rather than making the choice. Building this dashboard is itself a risk: the plan warns against letting it consume time the actual pipelines need.

What the Angel Eye finding actually changes

The recommended build order for Angel Eye is cross-session index first, then the MCP wrapper, then re-joining decisions. For the hackathon that order inverts. We control the project names, so every lineage already knows which sessions belong to it — per-session retrieval is enough, and the cross-session index is what you need to ask questions about a whole week, not about one build. The thin wrapper alone unblocks phase 05; decisions can be extracted at hackathon time from the events we do have.

Honest success bar

Five interviews, five fact sheets, five selections and five requirements packages — but only three to five runnable apps and one polished end-to-end demo. Ten finished applications is not the target, and pretending otherwise is the fastest way to lose the event.

Answers — what are we actually building?

The team interviewed by its own system

Lineage one is the pilot, and this is its discovery record — evolving, not final. Said is what a person actually stated; inferred is synthesis on top. Keeping the two apart is the same discipline every participant lineage will be held to.

The interviewer co-pilot

ETHON · BUILD SIDE
Said

An always-listening co-pilot for the interviewer. While an interview runs, it surfaces two things:

  • Useful information from the internet — data relevant to what is being discussed
  • Clarifying questions, proposed by the AI, for the interviewer to ask next

The problem: people don't know what they want. A good interviewer can extract needs, or get someone to realise a problem they hadn't named — but not everyone has that skill.

Inferred

This targets the weakest link in the whole system. The plan already names interview quality as the primary risk, and its diagnostic rule is that weak opportunity cards mean bad questions, not bad scoring.

It is also recursive in a way that helps the demo — the tool improves the process that produced it.

Downstream of the interview

KARLEN · HUMAN SIDE
Said

Once there are good questions and a working process for surfacing frictions and pain points, the next problems are:

  • Document all the frictions and pain points
  • Find patterns within them
  • Generate ideas — by a defined method, not intuition
  • Define what a good idea actually is

A good idea must satisfy the themes of the hackathon and the 20-point criteria. Together those decide which ideas are higher priority, worth building, or not worth building.

Inferred

This is the plan's opportunity-selection funnel, arrived at independently — a good sign the plan describes something real.

The sharpest part is "define what is in fact a good idea". The plan proposes ranking dimensions but never fixes a bar. Karlen is asking for the bar, and correctly places it outside the system.

Neither half works alone

A co-pilot feeding richer needs into a pipeline with no defined bar just produces more unranked ideas. A rigorous criteria set applied to thin evidence ranks noise precisely. They have to land together — and because they are owned by different people, the handoff between them is the thing most likely to break.
